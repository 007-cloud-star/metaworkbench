How to fill in a PRISMA 2020 flow diagram

  1. Identification. Start with the raw yield of your search: records from databases (e.g. PubMed, Embase, Web of Science combined) and from registers (e.g. trial registries), then subtract everything removed before screening — duplicates, automation-flagged records, and records removed for other reasons.
  2. Screening. The records you actually screened by title and abstract, minus the ones you excluded, gives the reports you sought for retrieval. Subtract reports you could not retrieve to get the reports assessed for eligibility in full text.
  3. Included. After full-text exclusions (list the reasons with counts), what remains is the set of included studies — and the number of reports describing them, which can be higher when one study has several publications.

Frequently asked questions

What is a PRISMA flow diagram?
It is the flowchart required by the PRISMA 2020 reporting guideline that shows how studies were identified, screened and included in a systematic review. Almost every journal that publishes systematic reviews expects one.

Is this generator free?
Yes. It is free to use and the PNG download carries no watermark and requires no account.

Which PRISMA version does it follow?
The layout follows the PRISMA 2020 flow diagram for new systematic reviews that included searches of databases and registers.

Can one included study have several reports?
Yes. A single study may be described by a protocol paper, a main results paper and a follow-up paper. PRISMA 2020 therefore asks for both numbers: studies included, and reports of included studies.

Is my data uploaded anywhere?
No. The diagram is drawn locally in your browser; nothing is sent to a server.
