How the overall judgement works

  1. Worst domain wins. The overall judgement follows the RoB 2 algorithm: High risk in any one domain makes the study High overall; Some concerns in at least one domain (and no High) makes it Some concerns overall; Low in all five domains makes it Low overall.
  2. Multiple "some concerns" are flagged, never silently upgraded. RoB 2 guidance notes that when a study has some concerns in multiple domains, reviewers may judge it High overall if the concerns substantially lower confidence in the result. Studies in that situation are marked † in the plot and listed under the summary; use the Overall selector if you decide to raise one.
  3. Overrides are yours. The RoB 2 tool leaves the final call to the reviewer (for example, when a domain's concerns plainly do not threaten the result). Any override you set is used consistently in the traffic-light plot, the summary bars and the counts below them.
  4. Summary bars, two ways. Unweighted bars count each study once. Weighted bars let each study contribute in proportion to its sample size N — closer to how much evidence each judgement represents. Studies without an N are excluded from weighted bars, and the note under the plot says how many.

Frequently asked questions

Is this the official RoB 2 tool?
No. This page only draws the plots (in the style of the robvis visualisations, McGuinness & Higgins 2020) from judgements you enter. The judgements themselves come from answering the RoB 2 signalling questions for each trial (Sterne et al., BMJ 2019) — this tool does not assess your trials for you, and it is not affiliated with the RoB 2 developers.

Which RoB 2 version does it cover?
The standard version for individually randomised, parallel-group trials, with its five domains. The cluster-randomised and crossover variants add domains (e.g. timing of identification/recruitment) and are not covered yet.

Why is there an Overall row in the summary plot?
Journals commonly show the distribution of overall judgements alongside the domains. It pools each study's overall judgement — after any overrides you set — exactly like a domain row.

Is this generator free?
Yes. It is free to use and the PNG downloads carry no watermark and require no account.

Is my data uploaded anywhere?
No. Both plots are drawn locally in your browser; nothing is sent to a server.
